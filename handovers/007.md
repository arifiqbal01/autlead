Autlead — Complete Project Handover

Handover date: 2026-08-22
Project: autlead
Primary language: Python 3.12
Package manager: uv
Database: PostgreSQL / SQLAlchemy async
Architecture: Modular monolith + ETL + replaceable provider adapters + bounded async workers
Current active product: WebArtsy

This is an update to the prior Autlead handovers, especially the 2026-08-19 handover. Earlier architectural decisions remain valid unless explicitly marked SUPERSEDED here.

1. Project Overview

Autlead is a self-hosted B2B lead-discovery, enrichment, analysis, and eventually outreach platform.

It supports multiple product/ICP strategies using shared infrastructure:

WebArtsy — web development, ecommerce, SEO, ERP, software development, branding, and related digital services.
Autply — planned AI-powered customer-support/unified-inbox product.

Autlead is deliberately a modular monolith.

The broad conceptual lifecycle remains:

EXTRACT
   ↓
VALIDATE
   ↓
TRANSFORM
   ↓
LOAD
   ↓
OBSERVE
   ↓
SIGNAL
   ↓
SCORE
   ↓

   ↓

   ↓
OUTREACH

Not every pipeline needs every stage.

The project deliberately integrates capable external/open-source extraction technology instead of rebuilding scrapers and detectors unnecessarily.

The current WebArtsy direction has evolved into:

        ↓
Company work queue
        ↓
N company workers

        ↓
PostgreSQL

A major current reliability priority is ensuring long-running discovery never loses hundreds of already-discovered businesses because Gosom times out or crashes.

3.2 Company processing uses an async worker pool

After discovery/persistence:

        ↓

        ↓

The reusable worker implementation lives under:

3.3 One worker owns one complete company operation

The conceptual target is:

A worker processes the company as a coherent unit rather than having independent application-wide stages for every provider.

4. Database Session Architecture

Current session factory:

SessionFactory = async_sessionmaker(
    bind=engine,
    class_=AsyncSession,
    expire_on_commit=False,
)

An AsyncSession must never be shared between concurrent company workers.

Worker-safe company entry point:

process_company_with_session()

Conceptually:

async with SessionFactory() as session:
    try:
        return await process_webartsy_company(
            session=session,
            ...
        )
    except Exception:
        await session.rollback()
        raise

The outer pipeline session can be used for non-concurrent operations such as discovery.

Each concurrent company gets its own session.

5. app/pipelines/webartsy/company.py

This module owns processing one already-discovered company.

Important distinction:

process_company_with_session()

owns session lifecycle.

process_webartsy_company()

owns the actual company business workflow and receives an isolated session.

The current implemented company stages include:

root website crawl/reuse
        ↓
business page discovery
        ↓
secondary crawl/reuse
        ↓
contacts
        ↓
people extraction
        ↓
people normalization/resolution/scoring
        ↓
decision-maker classification
        ↓
person persistence

The architectural direction now also requires:

technology detection
performance / SEO

to move into this per-company operation.

6. Major 2026-08-21 Decision — Move Technology and PageSpeed Into Company Workers

Decision: Technology detection and performance/SEO should eventually run inside process_webartsy_company().

Previous architecture:

discover everything
    ↓
technology for every website
    ↓
performance for every website
    ↓
company worker queue

Target architecture:

discover
    ↓
company queue
    ↓
worker(company)
       ├── crawl
       ├── technology
       ├── performance / SEO
       ├── contacts
       ├── people
       └── persist
Why

The old architecture creates giant barriers.

For 600 companies:

discover 600
    ↓
wait for technology across 600
    ↓
wait for PageSpeed across 600
    ↓
only then begin deeper company processing

That increases latency and makes failure/recovery harder.

Company-oriented processing allows company #1 to finish while company #600 is still waiting.

Alternative considered

Separate worker pool per provider.

Rejected for now because it creates unnecessary orchestration complexity.

What breaks if reversed

Autlead returns to batch barriers, slower time-to-first-complete-lead, harder recovery, and more complicated coordination between stages.

7. Important Clarification — Two Different Meanings of workers

There are now distinct concurrency controls.

Discovery workers

CLI:

--workers

Currently means Gosom discovery concurrency.

It maps to Gosom:

-c

Example:

--workers 4

means Gosom has concurrency 4.

Company workers

CLI:

--company-workers

controls Autlead's company enrichment worker pool.

Example:

--company-workers 2

means at most two companies are concurrently being enriched.

These are deliberately separate.

Do not silently make one flag control both systems.

8. Worker Retry Policy

Company workers support:

--company-retries

Retries must remain conservative.

Retry transient failures such as:

timeouts
connection reset
connection refused
temporary service failure
429
502
503
504

Do not repeatedly retry deterministic parsing errors or bad company data.

Do not introduce unlimited retries.

Backoff currently follows the intended pattern:

base delay
    ↓
exponential backoff
    ↓
maximum delay
9. Company Failure Isolation

A company failure must not terminate the entire WebArtsy run.

Failure model:

WebArtsyCompanyFailure

contains approximately:

company_id
company_name
website
stage
error_type
error_message

A failed company should:

rollback its own session
    ↓
record failure
    ↓
allow worker to continue

Missing websites are normally classified as a company-level skip/failure rather than crashing the entire run.

20. --limit Change

Earlier WebArtsy CLI versions defaulted to:

--limit 10

SUPERSEDED 2026-08-21.

The newer desired behavior is:

default=None

meaning unlimited application-side acceptance.

Why:

Gosom may spend substantial time discovering hundreds of businesses. Throwing most of them away because the CLI silently defaults to 10 wastes the scrape.

Explicit limits remain useful for tests.

23. Discovery Progress Logging

A significant operational improvement was added.

Instead of seeing only:

discovery_started

for 30 minutes, Gosom's CSV is monitored while the scraper runs.

Logs now look like:

gosom_discovery_progress added=1 discovered=18
gosom_discovery_progress added=2 discovered=20
gosom_discovery_progress added=4 discovered=24

Definitions:

discovered

is the current total number of CSV records observed.

added

is how many new rows appeared since the previous progress check.

For example:

added=3 discovered=37

means:

Three new records appeared since the previous check; 37 records currently exist in the CSV.

Do not interpret added as a company identifier or cumulative total.

24. Subprocess Streams Must Be Drained

Gosom stdout and stderr are continuously consumed asynchronously.

Reason:

Subprocess pipe buffers can fill and block a long-running child process.

Pattern:

stdout_task
stderr_task
progress_task
process.wait()

Gosom raw output can be DEBUG-level while CSV progress remains INFO-level.

Do not revert to leaving subprocess pipes unread.

25. Discovery Timeout Incident — Critical Learning

On 2026-08-21 a real discovery run reached approximately:

646+ discovered records

and then hit:

timeout_seconds=1800

The provider raised:

BusinessDiscoveryProviderError:
Google Maps discovery timed out

Because discovery had been performed in a TemporaryDirectory, the directory was destroyed as the exception propagated.

Result:

Hundreds of discovered businesses and roughly 30 minutes of work were lost.

This is unacceptable for production.

This incident drove the persistent discovery/checkpoint architecture.

26. SUPERSEDED — TemporaryDirectory for Long Gosom Runs

Earlier Gosom implementation:

with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory(
    prefix="autlead-gosom-",
) as temp_dir:

SUPERSEDED 2026-08-21 for production discovery runs.

Why:

A timeout, Ctrl+C, Docker failure, or Python exception deletes valuable partial discovery output.

Temporary directories are acceptable for tests or disposable operations, but not for expensive long-running discovery.

28. Configuration for Gosom Runtime Data

Settings now include the concept:

results_root: Path = Path(
    "data/discovery/gosom"
)

Important: Path must be imported:

from pathlib import Path

Do not type results_root as str | Path unless there is a concrete need. A Path setting is cleaner.

Provider bootstrap/configuration should supply this setting rather than scattering the path around CLI code.

29. Absolute Persistent Paths — Critical

The persistent results_root should be resolved:

self.results_root = (
    Path(results_root)
    .expanduser()
    .resolve()
)

Why:

The Gosom subprocess is launched with a cwd, and Docker bind mounts must refer to the intended host paths.

A relative path combined with:

cwd=work_dir

can make path interpretation confusing or incorrect.

Also resolve:

queries_file = (...).resolve()
results_file = (...).resolve()

Docker mounts should use absolute host paths.

This is especially important for debugging the current recovery issue.

30. Persistent Run Directory Design

Each Gosom run should get its own directory under:

data/discovery/gosom/

For example:

data/discovery/gosom/
└── 20260822_153012_accountantskantoor_amsterdam/
    ├── queries.txt
    └── results.csv

Exact naming can evolve, but requirements are:

unique enough to avoid collisions
human-readable where practical
persistent after process termination
usable for debugging/recovery

Do not overwrite one global results.csv.

31. Partial Discovery Must Be Recoverable

Critical production rule:

If Gosom produced usable records, a timeout/non-zero exit should not automatically throw those records away.

Desired behavior:

Gosom runs
    ↓
results.csv grows
    ↓
Gosom times out/crashes
    ↓
terminate process
    ↓
inspect persistent results.csv
    ↓
if usable rows exist:
    log partial failure
    continue with partial results
else:
    raise provider error

This turns timeout into partial success when data exists.

32. Current Recovery Logic Still Has a Bug / Is Under Verification

After implementing persistent output/recovery logic, another run failed with:

BusinessDiscoveryProviderError:
Gosom Google Maps discovery failed without producing usable results

despite earlier progress logging.

This indicates the persistence/recovery path is not yet proven correct.

Likely areas to verify:

Host bind mount path is absolute.
Gosom and Autlead are reading the exact same results.csv.
results_file.exists() is true after failure.
results_file.stat().st_size is non-zero.
count_results(results_file) can parse a partially written CSV.
Docker is writing to the intended persistent directory.
Process termination has finished before the final file check.
A concurrent write isn't temporarily causing read_results() to fail.

Do not declare discovery recovery complete until a forced timeout test proves it.

33. CSV Reading Was Separated From Gosom Provider Logic

The provider was becoming too large.

Decision:

Separate generic Gosom CSV/helper concerns from process orchestration.

Existing helper module:

app/providers/discovery/gosom_utils.py

Previously contained:

read_results()
parse_location()
deduplicate()
extract_domain()
optional_text()
required_text()

Direction agreed during the refactor:

CSV-specific operations should live in a separate CSV-oriented module.
Generic conversion/dedup/location helpers should remain separate from subprocess orchestration.
gosom.py should focus on Gosom execution, lifecycle, recovery and conversion boundary.

Do not put every helper back into gosom.py.

34. Partial CSV Reads Are a Concurrency Concern

Gosom can be writing:

results.csv

while Autlead is reading it for progress.

Therefore progress reads must tolerate:

OSError
UnicodeDecodeError
csv.Error

A transient inability to read a currently-written CSV should not terminate discovery.

However, beware this gotcha:

Returning 0 for every temporary CSV read error can make failure recovery incorrectly conclude that no usable data exists.

For final recovery, consider a short retry/read loop rather than a single fragile read.

35. Recommended Final-Read Strategy

For progress:

best effort
failure → ignore and retry next interval

For terminal recovery after Gosom exits:

wait briefly
    ↓
retry reading CSV a few times
    ↓
if rows parse successfully → recover
    ↓
otherwise fail

Do not use aggressive/unlimited retries.

The important distinction is:

progress read = non-critical
final recovery read = critical
36. results.csv Must Survive Timeout and Ctrl+C

Production acceptance criterion:

Start a discovery job, let it reach a known number of records, then terminate it.

After process termination:

find data/discovery/gosom -name results.csv -print

must show the file.

And:

wc -l data/discovery/gosom/*/results.csv

must show the already-discovered data.

If the file disappears, checkpointing is not implemented correctly.

37. Graceful Cancellation

asyncio.CancelledError should not be casually wrapped as a normal provider error.

Desired behavior:

Ctrl+C / task cancellation
    ↓
log run directory + discovered count
    ↓
terminate child process cleanly
    ↓
leave checkpoint on disk
    ↓
propagate cancellation

This allows the application to shut down correctly without destroying recovery data.

38. Gosom Process Termination

Timeout handling should attempt:

terminate
    ↓
wait up to ~5 seconds
    ↓
kill if necessary

Do not immediately kill() unless graceful termination has failed.

After termination, wait for process state to settle before making the final recovery decision.

39. Gosom Inactivity Exit

Gosom is currently configured with:

-exit-on-inactivity 3m

This is useful because discovery should naturally stop when Gosom has no new work/results for the configured inactivity period.

There are therefore two independent stopping mechanisms:

Gosom inactivity stop

and:

Autlead hard discovery timeout

The hard timeout should be a safety ceiling, not the primary normal stopping mechanism.

40. Discovery Timeout Strategy

A fixed 1800-second timeout caused useful work to be lost before recovery existed.

Once recovery is proven, timeout becomes much less dangerous.

Still, the intended strategy is:

normal completion:
    Gosom exits when work/inactivity is done


abnormal runaway:
    Autlead hard timeout


timeout with partial results:
    recover partial CSV

Do not make discovery timeout infinite merely to avoid handling recovery correctly.

41. Discovery Should Eventually Stream Into Persistence

Current discovery is still broadly:

42. Why Not Use Celery Yet?

A reusable async worker pool was chosen for current company concurrency.

Why:

already inside one Python process
workloads are async/network-heavy
simple bounded concurrency is sufficient
each worker can own an independent SQLAlchemy session
easier debugging
no Redis infrastructure required

Celery remains a future option when requirements include:

multiple application processes/machines
durable distributed jobs
external worker deployment
long-lived background scheduling
stronger distributed retry/recovery

Do not introduce Celery simply because workers now exist.

This updates the earlier "Celery later if workload proves necessary" decision; it does not supersede it.

Decision — Company is the primary enrichment work unit

Date: 2026-08-21.

51. Consistency Rules

Future work must follow these conventions:

Providers obtain external data; they do not contain WebArtsy scoring/business logic.
Pipelines orchestrate; they should not become giant files containing every implementation detail.
PostgreSQL is canonical.
Preserve raw provider provenance where useful.
Do not share AsyncSession across concurrent workers.
Company workers own company enrichment.
Keep discovery concurrency separate from company concurrency.
Reuse existing WebsiteContent; contacts/people do not recrawl independently.
Use structured logging for long operations.
Use bounded concurrency and bounded retry.
Do not introduce unlimited retries.
Treat external/provider data as untrusted.
Keep app/main.py thin.
Keep provider bootstrap simple.
Do not introduce generic factories/managers/repositories without a demonstrated need.
Do not create microservices or distributed infrastructure prematurely.
Persistent runtime files belong under data/; exports belong under exports/.
Resolve Docker host bind paths to absolute paths.
A partial Gosom CSV is valuable data, not disposable temporary output.
Test with real provider output, not only mocks.

These continue the project's longstanding preference for small modules, clear responsibilities, explicit schemas and minimal speculative abstraction.

55. Known Issues / Landmines
Landmine 1 — Never use TemporaryDirectory for production Gosom discovery

Doing so can destroy hundreds of results after a timeout.

Landmine 2 — Do not assume progress logging means recovery works

Seeing:

discovered=646

proves Autlead could read a CSV during runtime.

It does not yet prove that the final recovery path reads the same persistent file correctly.

Test it.

Landmine 3 — Relative Docker mounts

Do not pass ambiguous relative host paths to Docker while also changing subprocess cwd.

Resolve paths.

Landmine 4 — Partial CSV parsing

The CSV may be mid-write.

Do not treat one transient parse failure as proof that the file contains zero usable records.

Landmine 5 — Do not share SQLAlchemy AsyncSession between company workers

Every concurrent company requires an independent session.

Landmine 6 — --workers is not company workers

It currently means Gosom concurrency.

for company processing.

Landmine 7 — Amsterdam bbox is Amsterdam-specific
